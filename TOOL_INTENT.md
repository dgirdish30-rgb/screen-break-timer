# Tool Intent Statement: Screen Break Timer

## 1. Core Function
A simple, deliberately slow timer that counts down to zero and then makes a quiet, undeniable demand: *leave the screen for the next interval*. No punishment. No shame metrics. No tracking. Just: here is the time you said you wanted. Now honor it.

## 2. Human Context
You sit at a screen. Minutes pass. You did not decide to sit this long. The scroll is too smooth. The app is too ready-to-hand. You want to stop, but friction costs energy you don't have right now. The tool enters at that moment: it creates friction *before* you need willpower. It says: you set a limit. I am here to help you keep it.

## 3. Intended User
Someone (you) who:
- Knows screen time is harming your focus, sleep, or presence
- Wants to interrupt the pattern but lacks the friction to actually do it
- Does not respond well to shame or metrics
- Needs something that works *now*, not someday after behavior change
- Deserves dignity, not surveillance

## 4. Behavioral Thesis
Behavior change doesn't happen through awareness alone. It happens through *repeated small frictions at the moment of choice*. 

This tool believes:
- You will honor a commitment you make to yourself if the tool makes honoring it easier than breaking it
- Repetition builds new grooves; each time you step away, the next time is slightly easier
- The timer should feel like a friend, not a guard
- Breaking the pattern once is worthless; the tool must work the next 50 times

## 5. Dignity Clause
This tool will:
- Never shame you for going over time
- Never track or store your data
- Never send notifications or notifications
- Never gamify your behavior
- Never compare you to others
- Never use dark patterns to keep you engaged
- Accept that you will sometimes ignore it, and that's okay

## 6. Refusal Clause
This tool will NOT:
- Become addictive itself (no rewards, streaks, or social comparison)
- Lock you out of anything (you can always close it)
- Spy on your screen time
- Become a badge of identity ("I'm a 2-hour person")
- Pretend that stopping screen time is a moral victory
- Make you feel worse about yourself

## 7. Atmospheric Tone
Calm. Steady. Almost boring. Like a grandfather clock in the corner of a room. You notice it, but it doesn't grab you. When the timer ends, it should feel less like an alarm and more like a gentle hand on your shoulder saying, *"We agreed. Time to step away."*

---

## Design Constraints
- **One timer.** Not multiple. Not customizable intervals (at first). One clear decision.
- **Full screen.** When active, it takes the whole screen. No alt-tabbing away from it.
- **Visible countdown.** Large, easy to read. You can see time passing.
- **One action.** Start it. That's it. No pause. No snooze. Only way out is to wait or close.
- **Quiet ending.** A soft sound. A gentle visual shift. Not a screaming alarm.
- **No data.** What you do with the time after the timer ends is your business.
